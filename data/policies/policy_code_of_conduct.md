# Code of Conduct & Workplace Standards

**Effective date:** January 1, 2026  
**Owner:** People Operations & Legal  
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors, interns, consultants, and temporary workers, subject to their contractual terms.

## 1. Purpose

The Company expects every person working with us to contribute to a workplace that is respectful, safe, inclusive, professional, and free from harassment, discrimination, intimidation, retaliation, fraud, and other inappropriate conduct.

This policy applies during normal working hours and also to conduct connected with work outside the office, including business travel, customer meetings, conferences, company celebrations, off-sites, work WhatsApp/Slack/Teams groups, email, video calls, and other work-related communications.

## 2. Everyday workplace standards

Employees are expected to:
- communicate respectfully, even during disagreements;
- be honest in business records, timesheets, expense claims, and communications;
- protect confidential company and customer information;
- use company systems and equipment responsibly;
- avoid conduct that could reasonably make another person feel threatened, humiliated, bullied, or excluded;
- disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest through the prescribed process;
- cooperate with reasonable workplace investigations.

A disagreement with a manager, performance feedback, a rejected leave request, or a difference of professional opinion is not automatically misconduct. Concerns are assessed based on context and evidence.

## 3. Attendance, leave, and professional behaviour

Employees should follow applicable working-hours, attendance, remote-work, and leave procedures. If an employee is unexpectedly unable to work, they should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible rather than simply becoming unavailable.

Repeated unexplained absence, deliberate falsification of attendance or leave records, or misuse of another employee's credentials may result in disciplinary action.

## 4. Anti-harassment and anti-discrimination

The Company prohibits harassment or discrimination based on characteristics protected by applicable law. Examples include inappropriate comments, slurs, unwanted sexual conduct, repeated personal remarks, threatening behaviour, deliberate exclusion, or discriminatory treatment.

The prohibition applies to offices, remote meetings, business travel, company events, client locations, and work-related digital channels.

## 5. Prevention of sexual harassment in India

For employees working in India, the Company will maintain processes required under applicable Indian law relating to prevention and redressal of sexual harassment at the workplace, including an Internal Committee where legally required.

Employees may raise a concern through the designated POSH reporting mechanism. Such complaints are handled through the applicable statutory and Company process and are not resolved by an automated HR assistant.

## 6. Gifts, conflicts, and outside employment

Employees must not use their position to obtain improper personal benefits. Conflicts involving vendors, customers, relatives, investments, outside employment, or side businesses should be disclosed through the Ethics & Conflicts of Interest process.

The Company may restrict outside work where it creates a conflict, affects performance, uses Company resources, breaches confidentiality, or violates contractual or legal obligations.

## 7. Reporting a concern

For ordinary HR questions, employees may use HR self-service. For harassment, discrimination, retaliation, fraud, serious misconduct, or safety concerns, employees should contact People Operations, the designated ethics channel, or the India POSH mechanism where applicable.

Anonymous reporting may be available through the ethics hotline. Emergency threats to life or physical safety should be reported to local emergency services first.

## 8. Non-retaliation

Retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or supports another employee's complaint is prohibited.

A report made in good faith is protected even if an investigation ultimately does not substantiate the allegation. Deliberately false allegations may themselves be investigated.

## 9. Investigations and confidentiality

People Operations and, where appropriate, Legal will assess reported concerns. Investigations may involve interviews, documents, system records, or other relevant evidence.

The Company will share information only with people who need it for legitimate purposes, subject to applicable law and due process. Investigation timelines depend on complexity.

## 10. India-specific workplace considerations

The Company will comply with applicable central and state employment laws, including requirements relating to working conditions, wages, leave, maternity benefits, sexual-harassment prevention, and statutory holidays as applicable to the employee's location and establishment.

Where this policy conflicts with mandatory law, the mandatory legal requirement takes precedence.

## 11. Practical examples

**Usually acceptable:** A manager tells an employee that a deliverable needs improvement and explains how to fix it.

**Potentially inappropriate:** A manager repeatedly insults an employee in team meetings because of their background or personal characteristics.

**Leave example:** An employee is unexpectedly ill and sends a message to their manager before the workday begins. They can regularize the absence under the applicable leave process.

**Reporting example:** An employee witnesses inappropriate conduct at an off-site event. They may report it even though the incident occurred outside the office.
