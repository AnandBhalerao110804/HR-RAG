# Parental & Family Leave Policy

## 1. Purpose

The Company supports employees through childbirth, adoption, fostering, and the transition to becoming a parent. This policy explains Company-paid parental leave and how it interacts with statutory leave.

The Company will always apply mandatory local law where it provides a greater entitlement than the general Company policy.

## 2. Standard Company parental leave

Employees with at least 6 months of continuous service are eligible for **8 weeks of paid parental leave** following a qualifying birth, adoption, or foster placement.

The standard Company benefit is available equally to eligible birth parents, non-birth parents, adoptive parents, and foster parents, subject to local law.

## 3. Extended caregiver leave

An employee with at least 24 months of continuous service who is the primary caregiver may receive an additional 4 weeks, for a total of 12 weeks of Company parental leave.

Primary-caregiver status is normally self-declared. The Company may request reasonable documentation where required for administration or legal compliance.

Employees between 6 and 24 months who believe they qualify under a documented hardship exception may contact People Operations for case-by-case review.

## 4. India statutory maternity benefits

For employees in India, statutory maternity benefits apply wherever the employee and establishment are covered by applicable Indian law. Where statutory maternity leave is more generous than the general Company parental leave benefit, the statutory entitlement takes precedence.

The India People Operations team will determine eligibility and applicable statutory duration based on the employee's circumstances, including factors such as the number of surviving children and the type of qualifying event.

Employees should contact People Operations rather than relying on the general 8-week rule when requesting maternity leave in India.

## 5. Adoption and commissioning arrangements

Eligible employees adopting or becoming parents through an applicable assisted/commissioning arrangement may receive Company parental leave subject to the general policy and applicable local law.

Where Indian statutory rules provide a separate entitlement, People Operations will administer the statutory benefit.

## 6. Foster placement

A qualifying foster placement may trigger parental leave where the placement is intended to establish ongoing parental responsibility and meets the Company's eligibility requirements.

Temporary caregiving arrangements that do not constitute a qualifying placement should be discussed with People Operations.

## 7. Timing

Parental leave may normally be taken continuously or in up to two blocks within the first 12 months following the qualifying event.

More than two blocks require People Operations approval. Statutory leave requirements may limit the Company's ability to split certain types of leave.

## 8. Notice

Where the event is foreseeable, employees should provide at least 30 days' notice.

For an unexpected early birth, emergency medical situation, or accelerated placement, the employee should notify their manager and People Operations as soon as reasonably possible. Lack of 30-day notice does not automatically eliminate a statutory entitlement.

## 9. Pay and benefits

Company parental leave is paid at 100% of base salary and does not reduce the employee's PTO balance.

Health and other eligible benefits continue according to the applicable benefits plan and local law.

Where statutory payments and Company payments overlap, People Operations and Payroll will coordinate administration to prevent accidental overpayment.

## 10. Returning to work

Employees should coordinate their expected return date with People Operations and their manager.

The Company will make reasonable efforts to support a smooth transition, including workload planning, handover, and where available, return-to-work flexibility.

## 11. Nursing and lactation support

The Company will provide nursing/lactation breaks and appropriate arrangements where required by applicable law and Company facilities allow. Employees should contact People Operations for location-specific arrangements.

## 12. Partner and family support

The Company may provide additional flexibility, subject to manager approval and operational requirements, for medical appointments, newborn care, adoption appointments, or urgent family needs. Such time may be administered under applicable leave, PTO, or flexible-work provisions.

## 13. Documentation

Reasonable documentation may be required for payroll and statutory compliance, such as an expected due date, birth record, adoption/placement documentation, or other legally relevant evidence.

Employees should not be asked to provide unnecessary medical details.

## 14. Job protection and non-retaliation

The Company prohibits retaliation because an employee requests or uses a legally protected leave entitlement.

Job protection, reinstatement, and related rights will be administered according to applicable law and the employee's contractual terms.

## 15. Examples

**Example 1 — Planned birth:** An eligible employee in India informs People Operations two months before the expected delivery date. HR reviews the employee's statutory maternity eligibility and applies the legally applicable entitlement rather than automatically limiting the employee to eight weeks.

**Example 2 — New father/non-birth parent:** An eligible employee requests eight weeks after the birth of their child. The request is administered under the Company's parental leave benefit, subject to any local statutory rules.

**Example 3 — Unexpected early birth:** An employee's child is born earlier than expected. The employee's spouse/manager informs HR the same day. HR can regularize the leave without treating the lack of 30-day notice as misconduct.
