# Employee Leave & Time-Off Policy — India

## 1. Purpose

This policy is the central reference for common employee leave situations in India. It explains how employees should request leave, what happens during unexpected absence, how different leave categories interact, and how statutory requirements are handled.

The exact statutory entitlement can vary based on the employee's state, establishment, employment category, tenure, and applicable law. Where a statutory requirement is more favorable or mandatory, the statutory requirement takes precedence.

The HR portal is the operational source for an employee's current leave balance and available leave categories.

## 2. Leave categories

Depending on the employee's location and employment terms, the Company may provide:

1. **Earned/Privilege Leave (EL/PL):** planned time away from work, normally used for vacations and extended personal breaks.
2. **Casual Leave (CL):** short-duration personal leave for unexpected or routine personal needs.
3. **Sick/Medical Leave (SL):** time away because of illness, medical treatment, or recovery.
4. **Parental Leave:** leave connected with birth, adoption, fostering, or becoming a parent.
5. **Bereavement/Compassionate Leave:** leave following the death or serious family event involving an immediate family member.
6. **Marriage Leave:** Company-provided leave for an employee's own marriage where available.
7. **Menstrual/Wellness Leave:** where provided by Company policy or applicable local requirements.
8. **Disability/injury-related leave:** administered according to applicable law, benefits, and medical/workplace processes.
9. **Public holidays and declared holidays:** non-working days published on the annual holiday calendar.
10. **Compensatory Off:** time off earned for approved work on designated weekly rest days or holidays where the applicable policy permits it.

Not every employee or location necessarily receives every category. The HR portal and applicable local addendum determine the category available to the employee.

## 3. Planned leave

For planned leave, employees should:
- check their balance;
- submit the request through the HR portal;
- provide dates and, where required, a short reason;
- ensure important work is handed over;
- identify an appropriate point of contact for urgent matters;
- obtain manager approval before assuming the leave is confirmed.

Managers should respond within a reasonable period and should not leave requests pending indefinitely.

## 4. Same-day and emergency leave

Life events cannot always be scheduled.

Examples include sudden illness, a sick child, a family emergency, an accident, an urgent hospital visit, a bereavement, or an unexpected home emergency.

In such cases, employees should notify their manager or team contact as soon as reasonably possible. The normal advance-notice requirement is not intended to prevent genuine emergency leave.

If the employee is physically unable to communicate, a family member or colleague may notify the Company where practical.

## 5. Medical and sick leave

Employees should use the designated medical/sick category when available.

For longer absences, the Company may request reasonable medical documentation where permitted by law and necessary to administer the leave.

Managers should not request detailed diagnoses, medical histories, or unrelated medical information. People Operations should handle sensitive documentation.

## 6. Hospitalization

If an employee is hospitalized, the priority is recovery and immediate family support.

A family member may contact People Operations on the employee's behalf where necessary.

People Operations will help determine whether the absence should be coded as sick leave, statutory leave, disability-related leave, hospitalization leave, or another applicable category.

## 7. Family emergencies

An employee may need time away because a parent, spouse, child, sibling, or other dependent has a medical emergency.

Depending on the available leave category, the employee may use casual leave, PTO/earned leave, family-care flexibility, or another applicable benefit.

Managers should focus first on immediate safety and coverage rather than requiring extensive paperwork during an emergency.

## 8. Bereavement

The Company provides compassionate leave for the death of an immediate family member according to the applicable leave schedule.

As a baseline Company benefit, employees may receive:
- **5 working days** for the death of a spouse/partner, child, parent, or legal guardian;
- **3 working days** for the death of a sibling, grandparent, parent-in-law, or equivalent close family relationship.

People Operations may approve additional time using available leave or on an exceptional compassionate basis.

Reasonable documentation may be requested only when necessary for administration.

## 9. Marriage leave

Eligible employees may receive **3 working days** of Company marriage leave for their own marriage once during their employment.

The employee should normally request the leave at least 15 days in advance when the date is known. Marriage leave is not intended for repeated ceremonies or events unrelated to the employee's own marriage.

## 10. Parental leave

Parental leave is governed by the separate Parental & Family Leave Policy.

Employees in India should not assume that the general global parental leave duration is the maximum available. Statutory maternity benefits and other applicable Indian requirements may provide greater protection.

## 11. Public holidays

The Company publishes an annual India holiday calendar based on applicable establishment requirements and business needs.

Employees working across states or from different offices may have different holiday calendars.

If an employee works on a designated holiday, compensatory-off or additional compensation may apply depending on the applicable policy and law.

## 12. Compensatory Off

Compensatory Off may be granted when an employee is specifically asked and approved to work on a weekly rest day or designated holiday.

Comp-off should normally be recorded in the HR portal within the prescribed period and used within the applicable validity window.

Employees should not work on rest days or holidays merely to create leave credit without manager approval.

## 13. Leave during probation

Unless a local statutory requirement says otherwise, employees on probation may use leave categories available to them under the HR portal.

Probation does not eliminate statutory leave rights.

Managers should not reject legally protected leave solely because an employee is on probation.

## 14. Leave during notice period

Employees may request leave during their notice period. Approval depends on business continuity, contractual requirements, and applicable law.

Where leave cannot be taken, applicable accrued leave may be settled according to the separation process and law.

## 15. Unapproved absence

An employee who does not report to work and does not communicate may be recorded as absent without authorization.

Before disciplinary action is considered, People Operations should make reasonable attempts to understand whether the employee was facing an emergency or communication failure.

Repeated unauthorized absence may lead to disciplinary action.

## 16. Leave without pay

Leave Without Pay (LWP) may be considered when:
- available paid leave has been exhausted;
- the employee requests additional personal leave;
- a manager and People Operations approve an extended absence;
- another policy specifically permits it.

LWP may affect salary, benefits, accruals, bonus calculations, or service-related benefits depending on the applicable rules.

Employees should receive the expected payroll impact before approval where reasonably possible.

## 17. Combining leave categories

Different leave categories should not be casually substituted for one another.

For example:
- parental leave should not be deducted from vacation balance;
- statutory maternity leave should not be replaced with ordinary PTO;
- a genuine medical absence should be recorded under the appropriate medical/statutory category where available;
- public holidays should not normally consume paid leave.

Where two categories overlap, People Operations determines the correct treatment.

## 18. Leave extension

If an employee needs to extend leave, they should contact their manager and People Operations before the approved return date whenever possible.

An extension is not automatically approved simply because the employee has remaining leave balance.

## 19. Return from leave

Employees should return on the approved date unless an extension has been approved.

For extended parental, medical, or statutory leave, People Operations may coordinate a return-to-work plan where appropriate.

The employee's manager should focus on reintegration and workload planning rather than assuming the employee can immediately absorb all previously deferred work.

## 20. Leave abuse and false records

Examples of potential misuse include:
- submitting false medical documents;
- claiming leave while knowingly working elsewhere;
- falsifying attendance;
- asking another employee to mark attendance;
- repeatedly taking unapproved leave and later attempting to backdate it.

Suspected abuse may be investigated under the Code of Conduct and disciplinary process.

## 21. Manager responsibilities

Managers are responsible for:
- reviewing leave requests fairly;
- planning adequate coverage;
- avoiding retaliation for legitimate leave use;
- protecting employee privacy;
- escalating complex or statutory questions to People Operations;
- not inventing local leave rules outside the approved policy.

Managers should not provide legal interpretations of statutory leave. Questions involving maternity, disability, workplace injury, POSH, or other protected rights should be escalated to People Operations.

## 22. HR/RAG assistant handling rules

The Company's HR assistant may answer routine questions such as:
- “How many PTO days do I have?” when the system provides the balance;
- “How far in advance should I request vacation?”;
- “Can I take bereavement leave?”;
- “Which policy covers parental leave?”;
- “What happens if I need emergency leave?”

The assistant should **not make a definitive legal determination** where eligibility depends on individual facts or changing Indian/state law.

Questions involving sexual harassment/POSH complaints, serious misconduct, medical diagnoses, legal disputes, suspected discrimination, or statutory exceptions should be routed to People Operations or the designated statutory channel.

## 23. Examples for HR RAG testing

**Question:** “I woke up sick this morning. Can I take leave?”

**Expected answer:** Yes, notify your manager as soon as reasonably possible and record the appropriate sick/medical or other available leave category. The five-business-day advance requirement applies to planned leave and is not intended for genuine emergencies.

**Question:** “My mother is in the hospital. Can I take leave today?”

**Expected answer:** Notify your manager and People Operations as soon as possible. Depending on the circumstances and available leave balance, family emergency, casual, sick/family-care, PTO, or another applicable category may be used. HR can help identify the correct category.

**Question:** “I am pregnant and work in India. Do I only get eight weeks?”

**Expected answer:** Not necessarily. The Company's global parental benefit is separate from mandatory Indian maternity benefits. People Operations should review the employee's circumstances and applicable Indian law before confirming the entitlement.

**Question:** “Can my manager deny all leave during a product launch?”

**Expected answer:** Managers may consider genuine business continuity when approving planned leave, but they should not create blanket rules that override statutory or protected leave. Escalate disputes to People Operations.

**Question:** “I am on parental leave but still answering Slack messages. Is that okay?”

**Expected answer:** Employees on approved leave are generally expected to be on leave rather than performing normal work. If business needs are causing work during leave, the employee should discuss it with their manager and People Operations.

**Question:** “Can I work from Goa for a week while taking leave for the rest of the month?”

**Expected answer:** Working remotely during a leave period is not the same as taking leave. The employee should confirm the remote-work arrangement with their manager and follow the Remote Work Policy. If the location creates a cross-border issue, additional approval may be required.

## 24. Legal precedence

This policy is an internal Company policy and does not replace applicable Indian central or state law.

Where mandatory law provides a greater entitlement or protection, the law prevails. People Operations and Legal may issue location-specific addenda when required.
